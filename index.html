01

Start with the transformation

Many small inputs.
One discrete output.

A neuron integrates graded changes in membrane voltage. If that evolving state reaches threshold, it generates an action potential.

graded input→integration→spike timing

Speaker note · ~45 s

Open with the demo: each excitatory event was too small alone, but close inputs summed before the membrane leaked back to rest. Crossing threshold converted an analog state into a stereotyped event. This input–state–event transformation is the conceptual spine of the talk.

02

What is a neuron?

A polarized cell whose geometry shapes information flow.

Dendrites receive and transform input; the soma and axon initial segment integrate it; the axon carries output; terminals influence other cells.

ImportantThis is a useful canonical map—not a claim that every neuron has the same shape or one-way simplicity.
Speaker note · ~50 s

A neuron is a cell, not an abstract node. Its extended geometry matters: synapses at different dendritic locations are filtered differently, the initial segment is specialized for spike initiation, and terminals turn electrical events into effects on downstream cells.

03

The physical state

Voltage is separated charge across a very thin membrane.

Ion concentration gradients provide electrochemical driving forces. Selective channels set conductance; the membrane stores charge like a capacitor. Rest is a dynamic steady state, not “off.”

≈ −70 mVa useful textbook value—not universal across cells or moments

insideK⁺K⁺A⁻K⁺

Speaker note · ~60 s

The lipid bilayer separates charge, so electrically it behaves like a capacitor. Ion gradients act like batteries, and channels act like voltage- or ligand-controlled conductances. The sodium–potassium pump maintains gradients over time, but the moment-to-moment resting voltage is dominated by selective permeability, especially to potassium.

04

Computation before the spike

Inputs add, cancel, decay—and interact in space and time.

Excitatory postsynaptic potentials can depolarize; inhibitory inputs can reduce firing probability. Repeated nearby events sum temporally; distributed inputs sum spatially.

EPSP AEPSP BIPSP

integration changes probability and timing—not merely a binary sum.

Speaker note · ~55 s

Subthreshold potentials are graded and leaky. Their effect depends on sign, timing, dendritic location and ongoing conductances. “Excitatory” and “inhibitory” describe effects in context; inhibition can shunt current without producing a large negative voltage deflection.

05

A regenerative event

Threshold recruits channels that create the spike.

Voltage-gated Na⁺ conductance drives rapid depolarization. Na⁺ channel inactivation and delayed K⁺ conductance repolarize the membrane. Refractoriness constrains the next spike.

Na⁺ opens fast, then inactivatesK⁺ opens later
Speaker note · ~60 s

The spike is not electricity injected at one end of a cable. It is a local regenerative change in membrane conductance. The classic Na/K story captures the core mechanism, but mammalian neurons express many channel types and therefore show diverse spike shapes, bursts and adaptation.

06

The signal travels by regeneration

The action potential does not fade down the axon.

Local current depolarizes the next membrane segment. Myelin reduces current loss; concentrated channels at nodes of Ranvier regenerate the spike, enabling rapid saltatory conduction.

myelinnodelocal currentregeneration
unmyelinated
myelinated
Speaker note · ~50 s

An action potential keeps its amplitude because each segment regenerates it. Myelin changes the cable properties of the axon, and nodes cluster the channels needed for regeneration. “Jumping” is a useful shorthand—the intervening current still spreads continuously under the myelin.

07

Electrical → chemical → electrical

A spike becomes a probability of release—and a new postsynaptic current.

At a chemical synapse: depolarization opens Ca²⁺ channels; calcium triggers vesicle fusion; transmitter binds receptors; postsynaptic conductance changes.

Speaker note · ~60 s

A chemical synapse is not a perfect relay. Release can be probabilistic, receptor types differ, and synaptic strength changes over milliseconds to years. The sign of a synapse depends on the receptor and ionic reversal potential—not simply on the neurotransmitter’s name.

08

A phrase with many measurements

“Neural activity” is not one observable.

It may mean membrane voltage, spikes, firing rate, calcium fluorescence, synaptic currents, local field potential, EEG—or a hemodynamic proxy such as BOLD fMRI.

RuleAlways ask: activity of what, measured how, at what spatial and temporal scale?
µm · msmembrane potential
single cellsspikes / calcium
local populationsLFP / ECoG
cm · secondsEEG / BOLD
Speaker note · ~65 s

Different techniques do not record interchangeable versions of the same thing. Calcium imaging is an indirect and temporally filtered proxy for intracellular calcium; LFP and EEG reflect summed transmembrane currents shaped by geometry and synchrony; BOLD reflects vascular responses. Interpretation begins with the measurement model.

09

Activity becomes a pattern

Information can live in rate, timing, and population structure.

A single spike is rarely the whole message. Neuroscience studies spike counts, precise timing, bursts, synchrony, correlations and trajectories across many neurons.

rate how many?timing exactly when?population which ensemble?
state evolves through a neural population
Speaker note · ~55 s

There is no single universal neural code. Which feature matters depends on the circuit, behavior and readout timescale. At the population level, correlations can help or hurt information depending on how they align with stimulus-related dimensions.

10

The computational bridge

A neuron is a dynamical system—not a binary switch.

Its state evolves through time; inputs change conductances; nonlinear thresholds create events; history changes future responsiveness. Models choose which of these details matter for the question.

integrate-and-fire timing and networks→Hodgkin–Huxley ionic mechanisms→multicompartment morphology and dendrites
Hodgkin–Huxley ↗Computational text ↗
inputstatenonlinearityspikes
Speaker note · ~60 s

End by connecting biology and computation. A model is useful when its abstraction matches the question. Our opening simulator preserved integration, leak, threshold and refractoriness, but omitted channel kinetics and morphology. Hodgkin–Huxley answers a different question. Neither is “the neuron”; each is a disciplined compression.

About this project

A neuron, made explorable.

This independent educational project accompanies a ten-minute presentation on what a neuron is, how it works, and what neuroscientists mean by neural activity.

The interactive lab is a conceptual leaky integrate-and-fire model. Its spike waveform and propagation animation are explanatory graphics, not a numerical Hodgkin–Huxley simulation or medical tool.

No response or interaction data leaves the browser.
